# Portfolio & agency
Scope: personal portfolios, design/creative agencies, studios, photographers, illustrators, architects. Related: startup-landing, media-editorial, professional-services.
## Users & trust drivers
- Visitors: hiring managers, clients, art directors; skim 30-90 s on desktop, usually via a referral link.
- Trust built by a distinct point of view, real outcomes, named clients, visible process, fast high-quality media.
- Trust destroyed by template sameness, vague "we craft experiences" copy, broken links, slow heavy pages.
## Must-have patterns
- Work index: filterable grid or list with hover previews; title, client, year, discipline per project.
- Case study: problem, role, constraints, process artifacts, outcome metrics, credits; section anchors for skimming.
- Media: responsive AVIF/WebP, lazy load, keyboard-navigable lightbox, video with poster and captions.
- About/team with real photos and specific expertise; services stated concretely.
- Contact: visible email plus short brief form (budget range, timeline); current availability.
## Key screens
- Home/work index, case study, about, services, contact, journal/writing, photo series/gallery.
## Visual register
- The site is the portfolio: one strong idea executed with restraint beats many effects.
- Options: Swiss typographic grid, brutalist raw HTML, editorial serif with large imagery, playful motion-led, archive/index list.
- Typography as identity: characterful display face or strict mono/grotesk; color minimal or one bold field.
- Avoid tired default: "Hi, I'm X, I design digital experiences" hero, custom cursor blob, scroll-jacked GSAP everywhere, skills bento.
## Domain rules
- Honor prefers-reduced-motion; no scroll-jacking that breaks keyboard, back button, or screen readers.
- LCP under 2.5 s despite heavy media; alt text describes the work, not "project image".
- Respect NDAs and credits; label concept/spec work vs shipped work.
- WCAG 2.2 AA contrast still applies to experimental type and text over images.
## Anti-patterns
- Dribbble-style shots with no context, role, or outcome.
- Skill bars and percentages ("Figma 95%").
- Loading screens or intro animations before any content.
- Walls of tool logos; anonymous testimonials.
