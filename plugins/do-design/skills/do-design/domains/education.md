# Education
Scope: LMS, online courses, edtech, language learning, kids learning, university portals, tutoring. Related: productivity, media-editorial, wellness-fitness, gov-nonprofit, saas-dashboard.
## Users & trust drivers
- Learners (kids to adults), teachers/instructors, parents, admins; each needs a distinct view of the same data.
- Contexts: school Chromebooks, phones on commute, shared devices, low bandwidth; teachers juggling 30+ learners.
- Trust = clear progress, real outcomes/credentials, pedagogical credibility, child safety and parental control.
## Must-have patterns
- Course outline: modules/lessons with completion state, duration, locked/unlocked reason, resume-where-left-off.
- Lesson player: video with captions/transcript, speed control, notes, next lesson; readable text lessons with code/math rendering.
- Assessments: clear question count, save progress, immediate explanatory feedback, retry policy shown upfront.
- Teacher views: roster, gradebook table, assignment status, bulk feedback, at-risk flags.
- Progress: mastery or completion per skill, due dates with time zone, calendar of deadlines.
- Kids: big targets, audio instructions, parent gate for settings/purchases/links.
## Key screens
- Dashboard, catalog, course landing, course outline, lesson, quiz, results, assignments, gradebook, discussion, certificate, parent/admin view.
## Visual register
- Range: focused academic (Coursera, edX), playful character-driven (Duolingo, Khan Kids), crafted indie (Brilliant, Execute Program).
- Readable long-form type (17-20px, 60-75ch); restrained color for learning content, celebration reserved for milestones.
- Tired default to avoid: graduation cap icons, stock students laughing at laptops, lightbulb "aha" illustrations, crayon fonts for kids.
## Domain rules
- COPPA (amended rule, compliance April 2026): verifiable parental consent, data minimization, no behavioral ads under 13.
- FERPA for US student records; ADA Title II requires WCAG 2.1 AA for public schools/universities (deadlines 2026-2027).
- Captions and transcripts for all video; math accessible (MathML/aria); timed tests need extended-time accommodation.
- Grades and pass/fail never color-only; feedback specific and non-shaming; age-appropriate content moderation.
## Anti-patterns
- Progress bars that lie (100% after watching, not learning); points for clicking through.
- Walls of video with no text alternative; quizzes that only say "Incorrect."
- Childish visuals for adult learners or condescending copy; loot-box mechanics for kids.
- Dashboards overwhelming teachers with charts instead of actionable "who needs help" lists.
